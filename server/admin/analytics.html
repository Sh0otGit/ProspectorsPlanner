Brainstorm below. The two ideas that needed session-level usage tracking ("our own usage as a demand proxy" and "tool feedback vs. what people actually did") are the engagement panels above now that the instrumentation exists -- these four still need more scraped data, not more instrumentation, before they could be built for real.

Instructor rating trend

Once a few terms have been scraped, plot each instructor's adjusted score over time instead of a single number. Someone who's been climbing from 3.6 to 4.4 over three semesters reads very differently from someone flat at 4.4 the whole time, even though today's snapshot looks identical.

Needs: two or more terms of HB 2504 data for the same instructor. Have the schema for it now (evaluations.term_label); just need more than one term scraped.

Rating consistency, not just average

Two instructors can share a 4.2 average while one is reliably a 4.0-4.4 every term and the other swings 3.0 to 5.0. Surface the spread (e.g. standard deviation across terms) as a "how much does this depend on which section you get" signal, separate from the average itself.

Needs: same multi-term evaluation history as above.

Campus time-of-day density

With real meeting times/rooms for every scraped section (even without seat counts), we can show which time blocks and buildings are most saturated with sections campus-wide: a rough proxy for "everything is competing for a 10 a.m. slot" that doesn't need enrollment data at all.

Needs: nothing new. The sections table already has this once more than one department is scraped.

HB 2504 coverage map

An operational view more than a student-facing one: what fraction of scraped sections actually have a published evaluation, broken down by department. Tells us where the ranking is trustworthy and where it's mostly "no data" placeholders, and where to point the scraper next.

Needs: nothing new. A straight join between sections and evaluations once more departments are scraped.
